It was one of those days, the ones where the room wasn’t quite enough for some annoying and unimportant reason.

His first instinct was to look for others. Ask for help, speak to a real person, walk outside, look into another’s eyes. Any shared weight would alleviate the feeling.

His second instinct was to ignore the first. After all, we have been here before, have we not? You’ve already carried this weight. This time you simply got tired. Just wait. Do you remember what usually happens if you get too desperate?

Be quiet for a bit, won’t you?
Do you remember now?
Yeah, that’s it. A long, slow, deep breath, then move again.

It’s already better than before.
Remember that you are strong enough now, that was just a scare.

You can be a good weather friend now.
Someone will appreciate it, I promise.

It all reads as some sort of punishment, doesn’t it?
Maybe we speak from a place of delusion, to ignore our urges to cry for help, and to speed through the 7 stages of grief in a single moment.

And yet… it’s hard to think we would like it any other way.
To walk forward with scars is a lot more fun and aesthetic than walking forward in a sunny day.
Whatever cope it is, one learns to enjoy the bittersweet.
